About

About me

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Passau (Germany), supervised by Prof. Steffen Herbold, interested in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and interpretability. My broad thesis topic is targeted feature learning: unlike unsupervised methods such as Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) that attempt to decompose entire activation spaces into thousands of unconstrained dictionary features, targeted feature learning isolates and intervenes on specific, predefined concepts of interest. Specifically, I develop GRADIEND, which learns such a feature from model gradients in an SAE-like encoder-decoder approach. I applied it to a wide range of features, including German grammar, and made the method easily accessible as an open-source Python package.

As a side project I work on math NLP: MAMUT generates formula variations for training math-aware language models (the project grew out of my Master's thesis). Before my PhD I studied Computer Science and Mathematics at Dresden University of Technology.

Research interests: Mechanistic Interpretability · Explainable AI · Targeted Feature Learning · (Math) NLP
